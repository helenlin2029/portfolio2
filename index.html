Growing up

A sanctuary city of immigrants, driven by determination and work ethic.

I grew up in New York City, where my parents immigrated to from China. In the most diverse area in NYC, Queens, immigrants search for opportunities to improve the various aspects of their lives.

As a child, I spent much of my free time either watching PBS Kids, playing in my neighbor's backyard, or helping out at my uncle's restaurant. Commonly, many immigrants are foreign to the financial system in the United States and lack financial literacy. My first exposures to finance include discussing stocks with my sister, learning the concept of investing from PBS Kids, and conspiring on business models with my uncle.

When I was 10, my family moved to Michigan to start their own business. Now having spent all of my free time supporting my parents, I had exposure to the firsthand decisions that business owners made: the tradeoffs, optimizations, sacrifices, etc. This era of my life solidified my work ethic and influenced how I thought about making decisions.

At 12, we moved back to New York, where I eventually resumed my education following COVID.

Academic background

A technical trade first, then the theory underneath it.

Aviation High School

New York City · 2021 – 2025 · FAA Airframe Certification

For 4 years, I dedicated myself to a robust traditional education alongside the integration of trade education.

This form of education was the foundation to discovering what I really enjoyed; when I studied concepts in my classes such as math and physics, I would always wonder how the theoretical concepts would bridge with pragmatic applications.

In my trade classes (otherwise known as SHOP), I would go on to learn everything about airplanes, ranging from the braking systems, fuel systems, powerplant, airframes, avionics, and much more. Alongside building this foundation, I would learn how to physically maintain them using motor tools and documented references.

For 2 of those 4 years, I served as SHOP Foreman, ensuring that my classmates were on the same page, working safely, and clarifying any confusion.

After school, I served as the President of Key Club, Secretary of SkillsUSA, and doing summer programs (engineering and philosophy) at Columbia University.

2026

What I'm studying now

Two tracks, running in parallel: the engineering degree, and the quantitative finance I'm teaching myself alongside it.

On the engineering side I'm in the middle of coursework on algorithms, materials, and circuits, and in two research labs where most of the real work is pulling a signal out of a small, noisy dataset.

On the finance side I'm working through time series and cointegration analysis, state-space methods like the Kalman filter, regime models, and the discipline of valuation — reading, then implementing, then testing whether the implementation survives out-of-sample data.

The through line is the same in both: measure carefully, distrust the first result, and write down what would have to be true for you to be wrong.

Regime-aware arbitrage backtester

May 2026 – present · Python, Flask, pandas, statsmodels, hmmlearn, NumPy

A cointegration backtester spanning more than 100 S&P 500 tickers across 12 sectors, pairing ADF and Engle–Granger tests with Kalman-filtered dynamic hedge ratios.

  • Sharpe ratio of 1.33 on the strongest pair, SCHW/BK, across the out-of-sample period.
